The approach

A bodycam editor needs the context around the moment

A recording may contain long pauses, several speakers and important details away from the center of the frame. We work from your outline and source timestamps to identify the moments the story needs. The edit keeps enough context around each selection for the viewer to understand the order of events and the commentary being made.

Captions can support unclear dialogue, while speaker labels, timestamps and simple visual callouts help explain what the viewer is seeing. The three samples below come from the Bodycam / Crime homepage section. Footage selection and structure

Selected moments are arranged around your approved story outline. Tell us whether you have already made selects or need a review of the raw recordings included in the quote.

Dialogue people can follow

Volume balancing, selected subtitles and speaker context make conversation easier to follow. Narration, original audio and music are balanced around the intended viewing experience.

Context and agreed redactions

Dates, location labels, zooms and callouts support the commentary. Blurring, muting or removal of specific details follows the instructions agreed in your brief.

Build the brief

Brief your bodycam video editor

Share the source recordings, script or outline, voiceover and important timestamps. Include the number of hours of raw footage, planned final runtime and deadline. Provide the correct names, dates and any details that need blurring or removal.

You approve the factual account and source material. We agree how much footage review, dialogue work, captioning and graphic treatment are included. If there are multiple recordings of the same event, explain their order and any synchronization information you already have.

Before you start

Bodycam and crime footage questions

Do you edit bodycam videos for YouTube?

Yes. We can assemble source recordings and your commentary into a structured video, with agreed captions, context graphics, audio work and redactions.

Can you review hours of raw police footage?

Footage review can be included. Tell us the total source duration and whether you have marked the important moments, because review time is part of the project scope.

Can you add subtitles, timestamps and blurs?

Yes. List the required captions, time labels and redactions in the brief. We agree their extent before production and use your instructions for identifying details to remove.

How much does bodycam video editing cost?

The quote depends on raw-footage duration, finished runtime, captioning, audio work, graphics and redactions. The studio pricing options and a detailed footage brief help establish the right scope.
