The approach

How to use a video editing portfolio when hiring

Start with the sample closest to the video you are actually making. Notice how the opening introduces the subject, how the visuals support the narration and whether the picture and sound make the sequence easy to follow. Useful references point to particular moments, rather than only naming a creator or asking for the same effects everywhere.

The collection below preserves the nine Featured samples in their homepage order. To browse by a specific service, use the video editing service directory. It separates cartoon commentary, crime intros, bodycam, sports, explainers, health, gaming, named styles and promotional formats into their own pages with the relevant work.

Compare the kind of story

A narrated analysis, footage breakdown and short promo need different structures. Use a relevant example to explain what the viewer should understand and feel during your video.

Point to the moment you like

Reference an opening, a transition, a graphic explanation or a sound treatment with timestamps. Those details help turn a general style request into a concrete editing brief.

Agree a sample treatment

Build the brief

Turn a portfolio reference into your project brief

Send the sample URL and timestamps you want to discuss, together with your script, narration or raw footage. Explain what your own video is about, the intended audience, finished runtime and deadline. Include the graphics, captions and delivery versions you expect.

We confirm the footage review, asset sourcing, animation and revision scope in the quote. The reference defines a direction for your own material and channel identity. For a recurring series, ask about keeping the same visual language across uploads.
